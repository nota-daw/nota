Ember Graphite & Paper

Nota's UI runs on a warm palette — neutrals pulled to roughly 40° hue, lit by a single brass accent. Ember Graphite is the dark default: a DAW is used in dark rooms for long sessions. Ember Paper is the same system on a warm light ground — same hues, same roles, with brass darkened to bronze because against paper a mark needs less lightness, not more, to carry the same emphasis.

The concrete source of truth is src/managed/Nota.App/Theme/NotaTheme.axaml (both variants, in ThemeDictionaries), mirrored for custom-drawn views in Theme/NotaPalette.cs. This page documents those two files. Never hardcode a colour or size in a view — reference the token, or it is stuck in one variant.

01 — Foundation

Principles

Six rules decide almost every question that comes up. When a new screen feels wrong, one of these is usually being broken.

+ One accent, spent carefully

Brass marks primary actions, selection, focus, active state, and the time-domain data that is the point of a DAW. Everything else stays neutral. Green, amber and red are for status only — never decoration.

− No gradients or texture

Flat solid fills throughout. No background images, no glass, no glow. The waveform is the imagery.

+ Structure over shadows

Panels are separated by 1px Brush.BorderDefault lines. Shadows belong only to floating layers — menus and dialogs.

+ Dense, but on a grid

Controls are 22–28px, text runs 10–13px. A DAW shows a lot at once; density is the point. Spacing still steps in 4px.

− No emoji, no scale transforms

Icons follow Lucide geometry at ~1.75 stroke. The only glyphs allowed as text are the meta separator · and the transport symbols already in use. Hover never bounces or scales.

+ Mono means data

If a number is read, compared or aligned — position, BPM, sample counts, latency — it is set in the mono face. Prose never is.

07 — Track palette

Eight tracks, then it wraps

Track colours are assigned round-robin from Brush.Track1..8. They are muted and equal-weight on purpose: no track should look more important than another, and none competes with the brass accent. The two-colour rule

This is the one convention that carries the most meaning, and the easiest to get wrong.

+ Brass is the audio path

Anything the signal actually passes through — gain, frequency, drive, mix — draws its arc or fill in Brush.Accent. On a knob that means Accent = true.

+ Teal is modulation

Anything that controls rather than carries the signal — LFOs, envelopes, sidechain detectors, followers — uses teal #5B9E9C (Brush.Track5, aliased in the kit as Teal), passed via the knob's ArcColor. Group them behind a 2px teal left border with a teal section label.

− The output rail stays muted

Trim and pan at the end of the chain fill with Brush.BorderStrong, not brass. They are housekeeping, not the sound.

+ Tab accent follows the tab

An active tab takes a 2px left border: brass for signal pages (Osc, Filter), teal for modulation pages (Env, LFO, Mod).

09 — Control kit

What you build a card out of

Card bodies are assembled from a small set of custom-drawn controls plus the state-free builders in DeviceCardKit. Avalonia's stock controls are too chunky at this density, so almost everything here is purpose-built.

Interaction contract

Every value control in the kit behaves identically. Match this exactly when adding one — the consistency is the feature.

GestureBehaviour
Vertical dragUp increases. The knob travels its full range over ~140px of movement.
Double-clickRestores the parameter default (Default; NaN disables it).
Left button onlyRight-click deliberately bubbles through, so the CV-modulate context menu still opens.
Gesture bracketsGestureBegin / GestureEnd wrap BeginAutomationWrite / EndAutomationWrite, so a move recorded while playing lands as automation.
MIDI learnKnobs register through MidiLearn.Bind with the parameter's display name.
Live followA refresher re-reads engine state each UI tick and skips while Dragging is true, so automation never fights the hand.

10 — Visualisers

Every device shows its own state

Around forty custom-drawn views live in Controls/. The rule behind all of them: a device should draw the thing it does, not a generic graph. They share one ground — Brush.BgSunken with a #221F1A inner border — and read data straight from the engine on the UI tick.

Transfer curves — what goes in against what comes out

AmpCurve · CompTransfer · VintageViz · VelocityCurveViz. Input on x, output on y, dashed unity diagonal for reference.

Frequency response

AutoFilterCurve · DynamicEqCurve · EqThreeCurve · EqCurve · AmpToneCurve. Magnitude over a log-frequency grid. A dynamic EQ draws two curves — static and reacted.

Spectra

AmpHarmonics · OperatorSpectrumViz · CollisionViz. Harmonic magnitudes as bars — the amp pushes a unit sine through its own waveshaper to get real numbers rather than a drawing.

Scrolling history

AutoGainViz · CompGrHistory · CeilingScope · AutoShiftViz · FluxReactScope · ShutterViz · ForgeViz. A ring of the last few seconds. Gain reduction hangs downward from the top.

Waveform & sample

GrainWaveViz · RhythmWaveViz · StrataWave · AuroraStack · MonolithViews. Drawn in the app's standard waveform style so a sample looks the same wherever it appears.

Grids & sequencers

ArpGrid (16 steps per lane) · VoltMatrix (bipolar source × destination) · BeatRepeatViz (64 slots) · DelayTaps (echo train, L above R).

Envelopes & shapes

SynthViz · VoltEnv · ReverbTail · RhythmHitViz · LengthGateViz · DistributionViz · AutoPanViz. Where possible these are real — the reverb tail is an actual decaying impulse response, not a drawn curve.

The instrument drawn as itself

PendulumViz · FluxVectorPad · ChordKeysViz · OperatorAlgoViz. The strongest cards in the set: Pendulum shows its balls swinging across the triangular field, Flux gives you the XY pad to drag, Chord shows the resulting notes on a keyboard.

Icons

WaveIcon · GrainShapeIcon · AutoFilterIcons. Tiny custom glyphs — waveform shapes, grain windows, filter types — drawn rather than shipped as assets.

11 — Motion & voice

Fast and quiet

Transitions run 100–240ms on an ease-out curve, and they may only change opacity or translate by 6px or less. No scaling, no bouncing, no spring. A DAW is operated, not admired — motion that delays a click is a bug.

+ Layout skeleton

Transport bar → toolbar → body (on Brush.BgApp) → 26px status bar. Panels are Brush.SurfaceCard with a 1px bottom border. The body holds islands: the browser, the arrangement and the modular canvas are Radius.Md cards with a 1px Brush.BorderDefault edge, clipped to their bounds, floating on the app ground with an 8px gutter; the splitter between two islands is a 2px transparent grab strip inside that gutter and carries no line of its own.

Arrangement rows are not one pitch. A track row is 64px and its header carries every control (name + kind · mute/solo/arm + input · fader + dB + pan, with a level rail on the right edge). A group row is a 26px titled bar — disclosure, name, mute/solo, a 4px level rail, kind tag — and opens to a full 64px row only while it is the selected track. Header column 228px. A clip is a tinted body (Radius.Clip) under a 2px band in the track colour, with the waveform or notes across its full height; the name is drawn over the body, only where a run of clips begins.

+ Scrollbars recede

9px wide, thumb on Brush.BorderStrong, brightening to tertiary on hover and brass while dragged. The stepper arrows are hidden — at this scale they are noise.
