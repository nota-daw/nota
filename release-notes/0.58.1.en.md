Nota 0.58 is about racks and devices. Racks are rebuilt around one card and take MIDI effects, Mosaic and a whole Drum Rack; devices can be selected, grouped, moved to another track and copied with every setting. A sidechain can listen to a single drum pad, Windows gets ASIO, and every graph handle takes a typed value.

**1. Racks, rebuilt**
The Instrument, Audio Effect and new **MIDI Effect Rack** share one card: eight macros, Chains, Zones and Macros tabs, and the chosen chain's devices right on the card. An Instrument Rack chain now runs MIDI effects → instrument → audio effects like a track, and a chain can hold Nota Mosaic or a whole Drum Rack — a kit on one octave, a synth on the rest. Drop presets on a rack to start a chain, drag devices between chains, press Map and click a parameter to assign a macro, and use Mono and Glide on the Instrument Rack.

**2. Devices you can pick up**
⇧-click or ⌘-click to select several devices, then copy, delete, move them to another track or press ⌘G to fold them into the matching rack in place, sounding the same; ⌘⇧G takes it apart. The instrument is now a device too: select it, replace it with any other instrument or plug-in while keeping the clips, copy it or drag it to another track. Copy and paste keep every setting, ⌘D duplicates, and every device has its own × button.

**3. Sidechain from a single pad**
A key can listen to one pad of a Drum Rack, one chain of an Instrument Rack or one voice of Nota Rhythm instead of the whole track — duck the bass from just the kick, even when the kick is muted. Every sidechain picker lists the parts under their track, and there are 16 routes now instead of 8.

**4. ASIO on Windows**
Settings → Audio offers ASIO beside WASAPI: pick the driver and its channels by their own names and open the driver's panel to set the buffer, without a restart. Recording and monitoring share the driver's clock, and if the driver fails Nota falls back to WASAPI and says why.

**5. Type exact values into graphs**
⌥-click any graph handle — envelopes, EQ bands, filter pads, mod-matrix cells and more than twenty device graphs — and type its value in the units shown. Tab moves between a handle's values, and each typed value is one undo step and one automation gesture.

**6. See the sound better**
EQ-8 and EQ-3 get a real spectrum analyzer that reads pink noise flat, with Range and Peak hold. Nota Lens draws a tempo-synced scope with beat lines and a sidechain key overlay; Modular's Scope grows, pops out and freezes. EQ-8 bands take labels and notes.

**7. A tidier arrangement**
Return tracks take automation lanes, and return and master rows resize. Move clips with the arrow keys, drop them below the last track for a new one, and fold the piano roll to the scale's notes or a Drum Rack's pads. Devices remember their preset and mark when you changed it, and a plug-in's card shows a picture of its interface.
