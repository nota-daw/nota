Nota 0.55 is a new look. The interface steps back so your music carries the colour, and you can pick the colours yourself: four themes, including the warm Ember look of earlier versions. The new look, the themes, the browser's categories and the matching Nota Remote are the work of [Chris Laupama](https://github.com/chrislaupama). The arrangement learns to resize its tracks, the browser sorts devices by category, and Nota is lighter on the CPU, especially on Linux.

**1. Studio, a new look**
Neutral greys and one orange accent that means only one thing: playing, selected or switched on. Knobs, faders and device graphs stay quiet at rest and light up under your hand. Mute is amber, solo blue, record-arm red. The browser, arrangement and detail panel sit edge to edge on thin seams, the transport is a slim flat row, and clips are drawn in their full track colour. Device cards lose their inner boxes and share one header, so presets and power switches line up down a chain. Labels are in sentence case everywhere. Every feature, shortcut and menu is where it was, and your projects open unchanged.

**2. Colour themes**
Preferences → Appearance has four themes: **Studio Dark**, **Studio Light**, and **Ember Graphite** / **Ember Paper**, the browns and brass of Nota's earlier look on the new layout. Each is shown as a miniature Nota window, and picking one recolours every window at once. Choose **Follow system** to set one theme for dark mode and one for light, and Nota switches with your computer. Themes change colours only, never your projects.

**3. Resizable tracks**
Drag the bottom edge of a track to make it taller or shorter. It snaps lightly to Compact, Standard and Large. Drag with several tracks selected to resize them together, or hold **Alt** (**Option** on macOS) to resize all of them; double-click the edge to reset. A short track fits its name and buttons on one line, a tall one gives the input, fader and pan a row each, and clips and automation grow with the row. The track list itself is resizable too: drag the seam between headers and lanes. Heights and width are saved with the project.

**4. Category chips in the browser**
The Instruments, Audio effects and MIDI effects tabs have a row of chips: Synths, Drums, Samplers; EQ & filter, Dynamics, Reverb & delay, Modulation; Generate and Shape. Click one to see only that kind of device. Chips work together with search, Favorites and tags, and your plug-ins land in the same categories.

**5. Nota Remote matches Nota**
The phone wears the same theme as the computer and follows it live, without reloading or cutting a held note. Or set it to follow the phone's own dark and light mode. Its screens take the Studio look as well.

**Also**
• Arrangement, Session and Modular are now three icon keys in the transport, freeing room for the rest
• The Mixer window is as wide as its strips and resizes as you add tracks; Escape closes it
• Less CPU while idle: meters and the playhead redraw only when they change, and nothing redraws while the window is minimized
• The transport meter is now labelled **DSP**, which is what it always measured; its tooltip shows the CPU of the whole Nota process
• New tracks scroll into view instead of hiding under the Return and Master rows
• The Session toolbar fits small windows, and its inspector can be hidden
• Modular lists your tracks right after a project opens
• Linux: popping out the piano roll no longer crashes Nota
• Linux: settings live in one folder, `~/.config/nota`; files from `~/.config/Nota` move over on the first launch
