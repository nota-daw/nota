Nota 0.57 gives your voice a new instrument and your hands more precise tools. Nota Vocoder makes a vocal speak through a synth; the clip editor opens several MIDI clips at once; any knob takes a typed value; and the whole interface scales to your screen. Underneath, the engine now spreads a heavy project over several cores.

**1. Nota Vocoder**
A new audio effect with two inputs. Drop it on a vocal and you hear a robot voice straight away, from an internal saw at the pitch you choose. Switch the carrier to **Noise** for a whisper, or to **Track** and pick a pad or a synth to make it speak the words — mute that track and it keeps feeding the vocoder. 8–32 bands, **Formant** to make the voice smaller or larger, **Unvoiced** to keep consonants clear, **Hold** to freeze the sound, a live band graph whose edges you drag, and seven presets from Robot choir to Talk box.

**2. Edit several MIDI clips at once**
Select two or more MIDI clips, on one track or several, and double-click one. The clip editor shows all their notes on the arrangement's bars: fix a bass line split over several clips in one go, or write a melody while you see the chords of another clip. Select, move, transpose, quantize or run a clip tool across them, with one undo for the whole edit. A strip under the ruler picks the clip new notes go into, or locks a clip as a dimmed reference.

**3. An interface that fits your screen**
**Settings → Appearance → Interface scale** offers 75 to 200 %. Every window, menu, knob and device card follows at once, text stays sharp, and the choice is kept. Pick 125 % or more when the text is too small on a laptop, or 75 % to fit more tracks.

**4. Type the exact value**
⌥-click any knob, fader or slider (Alt-click on Windows and Linux), or double-click its value, and type it in the units shown — `1.2k`, `-6`, `12 ms`, `30L`, `1/4`. Tab moves to the next value on the card, and a typed value is one undo step that records into automation like a drag. Double-click still resets to the default.

**5. A clip editor that knows where it is**
Click or drag the clip editors' rulers to move the playhead, and grab the playhead line itself. The rulers read the clip's place in the arrangement — a clip at bar 9 starts at 9 — and switch to time with the transport read-out. The audio clip editor gets a **Tempo** field next to Warp, with **÷2** and **×2** to fix a half- or double-time detection, and the waveform stretches live while you drag a warp marker.

**6. More cores, faster scans, updates you see**
A project too heavy for one core now renders on several, export and freeze use every core, and an idle engine uses no CPU. Plug-in scans no longer hang on a stuck plug-in and get through large folders in minutes. And Nota checks for updates on every launch and daily, showing a new version in the status bar.
