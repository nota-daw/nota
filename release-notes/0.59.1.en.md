A release about recording and arranging: takes you can comp, a song you can rearrange by its sections, and a Mixer that lives in the main window. The piano roll learns every MIDI controller, and hosted plug-ins drop out far less.

**1. Take lanes and one-key retry**
Loop-record audio in the Arrangement and every pass is kept as a take. Open the lanes under the track and drag across a take to use that part of it: the comp plays on the track's own row, and Flatten makes it one clip. A take that went wrong is one press away from a fresh start: ⌘. (Ctrl+. on Windows and Linux) throws it away and records again from the run-up, leaving nothing behind.

**2. Rearrange the song by its sections**
Drag a section in the Sections lane and everything in it moves along: clips, automation and take lanes. The sections in between close up, and you see the new order before you let go. Move earlier / later, Duplicate with content and Delete with content are on the section's menu. New sections get automatic names that renumber as the song changes.

**3. The Mixer in the main window**
View → Mixer docks the full Mixer under the Arrangement, Session or Modular view, and it can still pop out to a second screen. Groups show as named, tinted blocks you can fold, and Compact strips fit about twice as many tracks. A group's Link key ties its tracks together: arm one and they all arm, and edits on a clip apply to the clips beside it on the other tracks.

**4. Every MIDI controller in the piano roll**
Sustain, mod wheel, pitch wheel, expression and every other CC now have lanes of their own under the notes. Add as many as you need, size each one, and draw pedals as on/off blocks or controllers as curves. The Bend lane gets a range menu, ⇧ + wheel changes velocity, and a note can be moved, lengthened or given a velocity in the same drag that places it.

**5. Nota in your language**
Settings → Appearance → Language downloads the interface languages from the community-run nota-translations repository. French is the first. Translators can add or fix a language with a pull request there, without building Nota.

**6. Plug-ins drop out less**
Hosted instruments are processed once per audio block instead of once per MIDI event, so dense parts cost far less CPU. On macOS, plug-ins that ran their own threads or asked the host for its name on every block no longer cause dropouts. The status bar shows the sample rate and buffer size (click to change it) and counts dropouts when they happen.
